How to Register Your Business for Free in Nigeria (2026 CAC Scheme)

Published by Rayglobal Digital World • November 8, 2026 • 10 min read

If you've been running your business informally, selling on WhatsApp, Instagram, or from a small shop, the Nigerian government has made it easier to go legit. The Federal Government, through SMEDAN and the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), is offering free business name registration for 250,000 eligible small businesses[1].

This is not a small opportunity. Registration normally costs ₦5,000-₦20,000 depending on the business type. The government is foregoing approximately ₦3 billion in fees to formalize the informal sector[1].

Here is the complete guide to applying, avoiding scams, and making the most of the opportunity.

Part 1: Why the Government Is Doing This

1.1 The Informal Economy Problem

Nigeria has over 39 million MSMEs, contributing about 48% of the country's GDP[2]. But the vast majority are unregistered. This means:

  • They can't access formal credit
  • They can't sign contracts with larger organisations
  • They can't participate in government procurement
  • They're vulnerable to fraud and legal disputes

1.2 The Strategic Goal

Formalisation unlocks economic potential. When a business has a CAC certificate, it can:

  • Open a corporate bank account
  • Apply for CBN intervention funds
  • Access BOI loans
  • Qualify for tax incentives
  • Register on e-procurement platforms

1.3 The Scale of the Scheme

The 250,000 slots are split into two categories[1]:

  • Business Name (BN): For sole proprietors and partnerships
  • Limited Liability Company (LLC): For registered companies with shares

Most applicants will receive the Business Name registration, which is sufficient for most SME needs.

Part 2: Who Qualifies?

2.1 The Eligibility Criteria

To qualify, you must:

  • Be a Nigerian citizen (18 years or older)
  • Operate a nano, micro, or small business
  • Not have an existing CAC registration
  • Be willing to provide accurate information (no falsification)

2.2 What Counts as a "Nano, Micro, or Small Business"?

SMEDAN's definitions[3]:

  • Nano: 1-2 employees, less than ₦1 million annual turnover
  • Micro: 3-9 employees, less than ₦5 million annual turnover
  • Small: 10-49 employees, less than ₦50 million annual turnover

If you're selling on WhatsApp or from a small shop, you're almost certainly in the nano/micro category.

2.3 Who Does NOT Qualify?

  • Businesses that already have a CAC certificate
  • Businesses in the medium or large category
  • Foreign-owned businesses (unless registered in Nigeria)
  • Non-citizens

Part 3: How to Apply (Step-by-Step)

3.1 Step 1: Visit the Official Portal

The only legitimate portal is: portal.smedan.gov.ng[4]. Do NOT use links sent by individuals on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Facebook. Scammers have created fake portals that charge ₦5,000-₦20,000 for "registration."

3.2 Step 2: Create an Account

Provide:

  • Your full name (as on your NIN)
  • Email address (functional)
  • Phone number (active)
  • Password (strong, unique)

3.3 Step 3: Complete Your MSME Profile

You'll need to provide:

  • Business name (the name you want to register)
  • Business category (e.g., "Graphic Design," "Retail," "Food Services")
  • Business address (physical location)
  • Business description (short, 2-3 sentences)
  • Owner details (your NIN, BVN)
  • Contact information

3.4 Step 4: Indicate Your Registration Type

When asked "Does your business have a CAC number?", select No. This tells the system you're applying for the free scheme.

3.5 Step 5: Submit and Wait

After submission, you'll receive a confirmation email. Processing takes 2-4 weeks. You'll be notified when your certificate is ready for pickup or download.

3.6 Step 6: Collect Your Certificate

Once approved, your CAC certificate will be available in the portal. Download it and keep it safe, you'll need it for banking, contracts, and future applications.

Part 4: The Common Mistakes

Mistake #1: Using a Fake Portal

Scammers create sites that look identical to the SMEDAN portal but charge fees. Verify the URL every time: it must be portal.smedan.gov.ng (with .gov.ng, not .com or .ng).

Mistake #2: Paying for "Fast-Track"

There is no fast-track. Anyone claiming they can accelerate your registration is a scammer. The service is entirely free.

Mistake #3: Using Inaccurate Information

The CAC certificate is a legal document. If your name or business details are wrong, you'll need to pay for a correction later. Double-check everything before submitting.

Mistake #4: Choosing a Generic Business Name

The CAC rejects names that are too generic (e.g., "Business Services"). Choose something specific and unique to your offering.

Mistake #5: Ignoring Your Email

All communication from SMEDAN goes to the email you provided. Check it regularly (including spam folder) for updates.

Part 5: What Happens After Registration

5.1 Bank Account Opening

With your CAC certificate, you can open a corporate bank account. This is required for:

  • Accepting payments from larger clients
  • Applying for loans
  • Registering on procurement platforms
  • Separating business and personal finances (essential for tax filing)

5.2 Access to SMEDAN Support

Beyond the certificate, beneficiaries are enrolled in SMEDAN's support ecosystem[1]:

  • Business training programmes
  • Grants and funding opportunities
  • Capacity-building workshops
  • Technical support
  • Market linkages

5.3 Tax Obligations

Once registered, you become taxable. This sounds like a downside, but it's not:

  • Small businesses with turnover under ₦25 million are exempt from VAT
  • Companies with turnover under ₦25 million pay 0% CIT
  • Proper accounting reduces tax liability (expenses are deductible)
  • Formal businesses win larger contracts, offsetting tax costs

Part 6: The Documents You'll Need

  • NIN (National Identification Number): Required for the CAC form
  • BVN (Bank Verification Number): For identity verification
  • Passport photograph: Recent, white background
  • Utility bill or proof of address: For business location
  • Business description: 2-3 sentences

Part 7: The Business Case for Registering

7.1 The "Trust" Factor

When a client sees "Registered in Nigeria: BN9326785" on your website or business card, it signals legitimacy. Unregistered businesses are viewed with suspicion, especially for larger contracts.

7.2 The Client Expectation

Corporate clients cannot legally pay unregistered vendors. Many will walk away if you can't provide a CAC certificate.

7.3 The Tax Shield

Registered businesses have legitimate tax deductions. Unregistered businesses pay taxes informally (often more than they should) with no legal protections.

7.4 The Growth Path

If you ever want to:

  • Hire employees
  • Take on a partner
  • Raise investment
  • Sell the business
  • Sign a government contract

...you need a CAC certificate. There is no shortcut.

Part 8: The Real Deadline

The 250,000 slots will fill. Once they're gone, registration returns to its normal fee (₦5,000-₦20,000 depending on type). If you've been considering formalising your business, do it now.

For businesses in Enugu, Anambra, Abia, and other South-East states, SMEDAN has regional offices that can assist with the application process. Contact them directly if you have difficulty with the online portal.
